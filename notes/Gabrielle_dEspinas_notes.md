# Gabrielle d'Espinas — research notes (6 Oct 2026)

Widow of Arnaud II de Roquemaurel, seigneur de Soueix (Couserans), killed in the burning of
Soueix c. 2 February 1567 by Jean-François de Mauléon, vicomte de Soulan, and Fontrailles.

## What the sources actually say about her

1. **Monluc to Charles IX, Agen, 14 Feb 1567** (Commentaires et lettres, ed. Ruble, t. V, letter 160,
   pp. 77-82; archive.org `commentairesetle05monl`). Does not name her. Attack "depuis douze jours en çà"
   (≈ 2 Feb 1567). "sa femme et quelque enfant qu'il avoit se sont jetés par quelque fenestre, laquelle
   ilz ont prinse et bruslé la maison, le dict Rocquemaurel et six autres personnes. Et l'on m'a mandé
   encores quelque chose de l'honneur de la pouvre femme, chose que de toute la sédition passée ne s'en est
   faict une pareille." Monluc says the Roquemaurel were Protestants, "bien apparentés de toutes partz",
   the elder had been maréchal des logis of the late king of Navarre (Bardies corrects: that was Girons).
2. **Baron de Bardies, "Les Huguenots en Couserans", Bulletin de la Société ariégeoise, t. IX (1903-1904)**,
   pp. 77-84 and 130 (Gallica ark bpt6k57278962, views 85-92 and 140). The only printed text naming her:
   "un arrêt du Parlement de Toulouse, daté du 24 mai 1567, qui condamna à mort Jean François de Mauléon,
   sieur de Soulan, et une quinzaine de ses complices, **à la requête de Gabrielle d'Espinas, veuve d'Arnaud
   de Roquemaurel, et du procureur général**." The arrêt was found by **Théophile Azémar**, former magistrate,
   Toulouse, "en dehors des archives de la Haute-Garonne" (so not in the AD31 registers). Family facts come
   from the **Malta proofs of Paul de Roquemaurel de Soueix, 21 April 1763**, manuscript held by the baronne de
   Roquemaurel at Oust, which also mention a "supplique par Jean-François de Mauléon de Soulan faite au Roi
   sur le sujet de l'incendie de la maison de Soueix et meurtre ... de feu noble Arnaud de Roquemaurel".
   Notarial details supplied by abbé Samiac (Marc Dupuy, notaire à Saint-Girons, acts of 10 Apr and
   1 Jul 1560; Roque, notaire royal, 14 Jun 1557).
3. **Roquemaurel family site** (http://tderoquemaurel.free.fr/degres_couserans.htm, http only), source of the
   Geneanet notice word for word. Adds: marriage "d'une maison du Comminges", "au moins un fils"; the
   arrêt's awards (5,000 l. furniture, 3,000 l. damages, 10,000 l. for the "pallais"; 1,000 l. to heirs of two
   priests, 500 l. to servants); "L'amende de 3.000 livres destinée à Gabrielle d'Espinas fut l'objet d'un
   procès qui durait encore en 1610" (no footnote). Footnote 26: "Extraits des registres du parlement de
   Toulouse ; copie AJR" (private copy). Alliance page: "d'Espinas alias d'Espinasse, ancienne famille noble
   du Comminges. La branche de Cardonne fut maintenue noble par jugement souverain du 2 août 1669 (Mirepoix)
   et du 23 septembre 1669 (Rieux)" citing L. de La Roque; arms "d'argent au pin de sinople, au chef de
   gueules chargé d'une croix d'argent". (La Roque's Toulouse volume, Gallica bpt6k206420z, gives no hit for
   Espinas/Espinasse/Cardonne in Gallica's OCR search; to be checked on the page images.)
4. **Lasnier, Fortifications médiévales en Couserans, 2008**, n. 177 quotes the arrêt (Lescure to be razed,
   expiatory column) from **M. Vuillier, Histoire de la famille de Mauléon, 1998, p. 52**, "extrait des registres
   du parlement de Toulouse, AD Haute-Garonne". Vuillier also wrote Histoire de la famille de Roquemaurel
   (2000). Both offline (CGLanguedoc library, Toulouse).
5. **AD31 printed inventory, série B (1903)**: register B 74 (1576), between entries of 5 and 7 July 1576,
   f° c. 190-195: "ordre d'exécuter un décret concernant Guillaume de Roquemaurel, malgré l'opposition de
   Jean-Antoine de Mauléon, qui est rejetée" = the site's "AD.HG B 74 p. 438". Checked on the images
   (see `Parlement_Toulouse_1575_1576/Roquemaurel_c_Mauleon_1576.md`): the order is 1 B 74 f. 198r,
   "Entre Guillaume de Roquemaurel s.r de Suys demandeur en execution de decret ... et Jehan Anthoine de
   Mauleon ... opposant", citing "le decret obtenu de la Court par led. Roquemaurel du xxv de Juing mil v.c
   lxxv"; the opposition is dismissed. The decree itself is not in the 25 June 1575 sitting of 1 B 72
   (B 73 starts only in November 1575).
6. **Not found anywhere**: her parents, siblings, remarriage, death, the son's name. The 1610 lawsuit is the
   last trace. 18th-c. "d'Espinas" and "de Lespinasse" families at Montesquieu-Volvestre (diocese of Rieux,
   AD31 série C vingtième rolls) may be the same Comminges family.

## Update — the Espinas family of Cardonne (checked in the original rolls)

- **Catalogue général des gentilshommes de la province de Languedoc** (Henry de Caux, Pézenas, 1676;
  archive.org `bub_gb_7sA_AAAAQAAJ`), the printed list of the Bezons nobility judgments of 1668-1671:
  - "Du 2. Aoust 1669. Nobles Gabriel Despinas Sieur de Cardonne, & Jean-Auguste Despinas Sieur dudit
    Cardonne, Diocese de Rieux, leurs titres de Noblesse ont esté confirmez par Iugement Souverain,
    Monsieur Villerase Rapporteur." A second entry of the same day places Gabriel Despinas "resident audit
    lieu Diocese de Mirepoix".
  - Under the diocese of Mirepoix, a September judgment (year unreadable in the OCR): "Noble Espinasse
    Sieur de Cardonne ... porte pour Armes d'argent, à un Pin de sinople, en chef de gueules, à une Croix
    d'argent."
- **Brémond, Nobiliaire toulousain** (1863; archive.org `nobiliairetoulou00br`): "Despinas ou Espinas
  (Gabriel et Jean-Auguste), sieur de Cardonne au diocèse de Rieux, furent maintenus dans leur noblesse par
  jugement souverain, rendu par M. de Besons, le 2 août 1669. Leurs descendants furent confirmés nobles de
  race et comme seigneurs de Cardonne et de Gousens, par décision de M. Sanson, intendant de Montauban, le
  29 mars 1697." Arms: "D'argent, à l'aubépin de sinople ; au chef de gueules, chargé de deux croisettes
  d'argent." Jean-Jacques Despinasse, seigneur de Gouzens, sat in the Comminges and Nébouzan noble assembly
  at Muret in 1789.
- **What this does and does not prove.** It locates a noble Espinas family in the Volvestre (Gouzens,
  diocese of Rieux), on the Comminges border, near Montesquieu-Volvestre where an 18th-c. "d'Espinas" also
  appears. That fits "d'une maison du Comminges". It gives no link to Gabrielle a century earlier. The
  recurring name Gabriel is only a weak hint. The filiation would sit in the 1669 proofs (Bezons
  procès-verbaux, AD Hérault / AD Haute-Garonne C) or the 1697 Sanson decision (AD Tarn-et-Garonne).

## Update — Soulan himself

**"Démembrement de la vicomté de Couserans"**, Bulletin de la Société ariégeoise, a volume after t. IX
(Gallica bpt6k5405682f, pp. 57-58), from the Sentein family papers on Soulan:
- Jean-François de Mauléon was the only son of Raymond-Roger de Mauléon (died 1555-1557) and Marguerite de
  Lordat. That makes "Goras" Arnaud de Lordat, one of the arsonists, very likely a kinsman.
- He married Anne d'Astarac by contract of 8 May 1560. She was the daughter of Jean-Jacques d'Astarac,
  seigneur de Fontrailles et de Marestaing, and Anne de Narbonne. That is the "beau-frère Fontrailles" and
  explains Marestaing among the accused. The couple had no children.
- He was still alive on 5 June 1571, when he made a last accord with the inhabitants of Soulan. So he did
  not die at Lunel in 1570.
- Bardies's 1903 article is titled "Les guerres de religion en Couserans", BSA t. IX, from p. 77.

## Update — where the 24 May 1567 sentence (and Gabrielle's damages) should be

- **Tournelle criminal arrêts (1 B 3356-3883):** 1 B 3456 = 1567 (février-26 mars), 1 B 3457 = 1567
  (2 juin-juillet). The cotes are consecutive, so **the April-May 1567 criminal register is lost**.
- **Grand Chambre parchment arrêts:** 1 B 60 ends 13 May 1567, 1 B 61 starts 2 June 1567. Same gap.
- **Grand Chambre paper duplicates, "Arrêts en double" (1 B 1976-2296):** **1 B 2103 = 1567 (avril-mai)**
  survives. Accused gentlemen could claim judgment by the Grand Chambre, so the sentence against Soulan
  may be copied here. Not digitised (no images in the catalogue; IIIF server has none). Reading room only.
- The 1576 order (1 B 74 f. 198r) says the decree is to be "executé en ce qui reste entre les opposans".
  In a sale by decree the "opposants" are creditors claiming on the price. Gabrielle's 18,000 livres of
  damages would make her such a creditor, but the entry names no one but Roquemaurel and Mauléon. This is an
  inference, not evidence that she was paid.
- Nothing found anywhere shows her actually receiving any of the 18,000 livres. The only later trace is
  the family history's "procès qui durait encore en 1610" over the 3,000 livres.

## Deeper on the Espinas de Cardonne (Geneanet, 7 Oct. 2026)

- **Achille's marriage, primary reference.** *Inventaire des archives de Ginestet (Languedoc)*, Y. Chassin
  du Guerny (Geneanet library, viewer 540182, PDF p. 64), Saint-Jean d'Honoux papers: "GG mariage entre
  noble Achyle Despyrias, seigneur de Cardonne et damoiselle Viollant fille de noble Antoine de
  Saint-Jehan, sgr d'Honnous, Michel Souchon, notaire de Fanjaux 13 mai 1566". Same inventory: Antoine
  de Saint-Jean, sgr d'Hounoux, 1564-65; his son Jean-François married Françoise de Thury.
- Achille was already "seigneur de Cardonne" in May 1566, so his father Pierre was dead by then
  (Pierre's testament 4 Jan. 1560).
- Lézat parish (AD Ariège, indexed): Despinas families at Lézat 1589-1735; Bertrand Despinas with
  "demoiselle" wives 1602 and 1611 (= Jean-Bertrand, sgr de Cardonne); Jean-Gabriel Despinas married to
  Marie-Thérèse de Gouzens (children 1685-1702); Gaspard Despinas de Cardonne, 1733 (Société historique de
  Gascogne). So Cardonne and Gouzens are the same family.
- **Date checks:**
  - Achille married 13 May 1566, so his children were born from 1567. Gabrielle was a mother before
    Feb. 1567, so **Achille cannot be her father**.
  - Pierre was a confrère in 1528, so born about 1500-1508 or earlier; testament 1560. A daughter born
    about 1530-1548 fits (Pierre aged about 22-48). **Pierre as father: chronologically possible.**
  - Achille married 1566, so born about 1535-1545: **a sibling of Gabrielle, consistent**.
  - Generations check: François (testament 1585), then Jean-Bertrand (children 1602-1611, emancipated
    son 1645), then Gabriel (baptised 1611, maintained 1669, aged 58). Consistent.
  - Aubais prints Claire Espinas's marriage to François Galauba as "5 sept. 1509", but François's
    father married in 1572. The date must be 1609 (misprint).
- Still unproven: no document seen names Gabrielle as Pierre's daughter.

## Religion of Gabrielle (assessment)

No document states her religion. Evidence, strongest first:
1. **Two priests were killed in the château de Soueix** (the 1567 arrêt awards 1,000 l. to "les héritiers
   des deux prêtres assassinés dans le château de Soueix"). Priests living in or visiting the house
   point to Catholic practice in her household in 1567.
2. **She prosecuted jointly with the procureur général before the Parlement of Toulouse** (Bardies),
   the most militantly Catholic court in the south. That alliance fits a Catholic plaintiff, though it
   does not prove it.
3. **Her probable birth family was Catholic.** Pierre Espinas was a confrère of N.-D. de Bethléem at Lézat
   in 1528 (before the Reformation reached the region). The family's children were baptised in the
   Catholic parish of Lézat from 1602. Her brother Achille married in 1566 at Fanjeaux into the Saint-Jean
   d'Honoux, a Toulouse parlementary family whose captain d'Honoux served Catholic commanders (e.g. with
   the vicomte de Mirepoix, 1579).
4. **The Roquemaurel side is contradictory.** Monluc, 14 Feb. 1567: "les Rocquemaurelz, qui sont aussi
   de la nouvelle religion". But his editor Ruble cites the Pamiers account presenting the Roquemaurel as
   leaders of the Catholic party, and indexes "Roquemaurel (s. de), gentilh. cath.". The Protestant
   *Discours des troubles de Pamiers* (Archives curieuses, 1re s., t. VI, pp. 329-341) shows "ce
   Roquemantel" leading Tilladet's royal troops in 1567 "contre ceux de Pamies qui s'estoyent retirez aux
   Cabanes". Lestrade: only Girons was Protestant (and Girons was with the religionnaires in 1573).
**Conclusion:** most probably Catholic, and Catholic from birth (born c. 1530-48 into a Catholic family
in a Catholic parish, before Protestantism took hold in the county of Foix in the 1560s). No trace of a
conversion. "Since when" cannot be dated more precisely than her baptism.

## The two priests killed at Soueix (what is known)

- **Only source:** the Roquemaurel family history's summary of the arrêt of 24 May 1567, made from a
  private copy ("Extraits des registres du parlement de Toulouse ; copie AJR"): "les héritiers des deux
  prêtres assassinés dans le château de Soueix recevaient 1.000 livres, ceux des serviteurs et
  chambrières d'Arnaud de Roquemaurel 500 livres."
- The priests are not named in any published text found. Bardies, Lestrade, Monluc, Lasnier, the
  Ariège archives database and Gallica's full text do not mention them.
- **Count of the dead is inconsistent:**
  - Monluc (14 Feb. 1567): "bruslé la maison, le dict Rocquemaurel et six autres personnes", i.e. seven dead.
  - The family history says six servants and chambermaids "précipités du haut du château", and its
    footnote 28 adds: "La liste fait apparaître sept personnes, le texte dit six". So the arrêt names the
    victims.
  - If the two priests are among Monluc's six, the dead would be Arnaud, two priests and four or five
    servants. The arrêt's list would settle it.
- "Héritiers" means the priests left families with claims, so they were secular clergy (chaplains of
  the château or priests of the parish of Soueix), not monks. This is an inference.
- Monluc's letter also says Fontrailles's band attacked "gens d'esglise" in general and seized
  benefices by force (complaint of the officers of the bishop of Comminges).
- **Where the names would be:** the arrêt itself (register 1 B 2103, AD Haute-Garonne, the Grand
  Chambre paper duplicates for April-May 1567); Vuillier, *Histoire de la famille de Mauléon* (1998),
  p. 52 ff., which transcribes the arrêt; the Roquemaurel family's private copy; Couserans diocesan
  collations of 1567 (benefices vacated "per obitum"), if any survive at AD Ariège (series G).

## Gabrielle and Monluc

- No personal or family tie found. Monluc was the king's lieutenant-general in Guyenne.
- 14 Feb. 1567 (Agen, letter 160 to Charles IX, Ruble V pp. 77-82): he reports the night attack about
  twelve days earlier, "dont sa femme et quelque enfant qu'il avoit se sont jetés par quelque fenestre",
  and "l'on m'a mandé encores quelque chose de l'honneur de la pouvre femme, chose que de toute la
  sédition passée ne s'en est faict une pareille". He asks for troops, artillery, and the razing of
  Fontrailles's and Soulan's houses.
- 31 March 1567: asks the capitouls of Toulouse for two culverins "pour marcher contre Solan et
  Fonterailles" (refused). Receives the king's commission; sends Tilladet with about 300 arquebusiers;
  goes in person to Saint-Girons and Saint-Lizier. ("Tilladet" here is most probably **François de
  Cassagnet, seigneur de Saint-Orens, "Tilladet le jeune"**, colonel of the légion de Guyenne, named in
  the Comminges petition of 1567, not his elder brother Antoine, killed at Mont-de-Marsan in 1569. See
  `Tilladet.md`.)
- 11 July 1567 (Cassaigne) and 7 Sept. 1567 (Agen): rules on the costs of the expedition between
  Comminges and Saint-Girons.
- He never names Gabrielle; to him she is "la pouvre femme". The Parlement, not Monluc, gave her damages.

## Guillaume de Roquemaurel, his wife and his testament

Sources: family history (tderoquemaurel.free.fr, degres_couserans and montegut pages); Cabinet d'Hozier
298, Malta proofs abstract of 1677 (Gallica btv1b100803203, images 142-143); BnF Français 32298,
Nobiliaire des généralités de Montauban et d'Auch, judgment of 21 June 1698 (Gallica btv1b10720544j,
images 81-83, saved in `BnF_Fr32298_Nobiliaire_Montauban/`); AD31 1 B 74 f. 198r.

- Born c. 1518, second son of Jean de Roquemaurel and Catherine d'Ustou. Substituted to his elder brother
  Arnaud in their father's testament of 27 Oct. 1552 (Cabalby and Goudan, notaries of Vic), which is how he
  became seigneur de Soueix after Arnaud's death in 1567.
- Ensign of the company of the king's ordonnances; at the siege of Metz 1552-53 (Duclos).
- 14 June 1555, living at Oust, witness to an accord between the people of Espou and Soulan himself.
- House in rue Sainte-Geme, Saint-Girons, against the town wall, 1561 (Samiac).
- **Marriage:** 2 Dec. 1553 to **Françoise de Roques**, daughter of Jacques Roques of Saint-Girons and
  Guillaumette alias Gaudiette de Méritens (contract before Dupuy, notary at Saint-Girons; family history
  citing BnF Fr. 32298 and AD Tarn-et-Garonne C 95). The 1677 Malta abstract dates her "1560" in the margin.
  Roques is not styled noble: a Saint-Girons family. The Roques inheritance passed to her children
  (receipts of 1595 for "les héritiers de feu Jacques Roques"; Ysabeau's 1,000 l. "partie de la succession
  d'Arnaud Roques, héritier de feu Jacques Roques son aïeul").
- 10 May 1567: already seigneur de Soueix (BnF Pièces originales 2544).
- 22 March 1569, Metz: letters of provision as gentilhomme ordinaire de la chambre du roi "en
  consideration des bons services qu'il avoit rendus a Sa Majesté au fait des guerres dans la fonction de
  sa charge", signed by the king and Neufville, red wax (Malta abstract).
- **Testament, 22 January 1570**, before Dis, royal notary of Oust: "par lequel apres avoir fait plusieurs
  legs, il institüe son heritier noble Marcantoine de Roquemaurel, son fils, et de Dlle Françoise de Roques,
  sa feme" (Malta abstract, f. 2v). Made after the 1569 campaign season, before the 1570 fighting.
- 10 March 1570/71: Soulan's 1567 petition to the king about the fire and the murder served on him,
  "parlant a Dlle Françoise de Roques sa femme", by Comparie, commissaire (Malta abstract).
- 25 June 1575: decree of the Parlement adjudicating to him half of Soulan's share of Soulan, Erp, Araux
  and Regule; 6 July 1576 the court orders it executed over Jean-Antoine de Mauléon's opposition
  (1 B 74 f. 198r, "Guillaume de Roquemaurel s.r de Suys demandeur en execution de decret").
- 1580-81: 250 écus sols on the 500 écus granted by the king for 1578 as gentilhomme de la chambre.
- **New (Fr. 32298, 1698 judgment):** after his brother Girons (Hiérôme), enseigne of fifty men-at-arms,
  died, Guillaume administered Girons's estate as paternal uncle of the heir Octavien. A transaction
  "pour raison de l'administration des biens de la succession dud. Hierome faite par Guillaume de
  Roquemaurel oncle paternel dud. Octavien et pere dud. Marc Antoine" was passed between Octavien and
  Marc-Antoine on 8 May 1598 before Devic, notary of Couserans. Guillaume was dead by then (family history:
  before 1597).
- **Marriage contract confirmed (Fr. 32298, images 378-379):** judgment of Le Gendre, intendant of
  Montauban, 22 April 1700, maintaining Octavien de Roquemaurel, sieur de Montastruc (Jean-Paul's line),
  cites "Contract de Mariage dud. Noble Guillaume de Roquemaurel avec Françoise de Roques du deux
  decembre mil cinq cens cinquante trois", after Jean's 1552 testament "qui fait mention de Catherine
  d'Uston sa femme et de Guillaume de Roquemaurel l'un de ses fils", then "Contract de Mariage de Noble
  Jean Paul de Roquemaurel fils dud. Noble Guillaume ... avec Demoizelle Marguerite de Barthe du vingt
  quatre fevrier mil six cent deux".
- Children: Marc-Antoine (c. 1565, married Paule d'Ustou de la Molette 24 Jan. 1594, Montégut branch);
  Jean-Paul (Montastruc, then Taurignan branch); Ysabeau (married Jean-Roger de Méritens 11 Feb. 1613).
- Religion: Monluc (1567) calls the Roquemaurel Protestants; Lestrade says only Girons was; Bardies first
  took the Protestant "capitaine de Roquemaurel" of Montbrun (1573) for Guillaume, then corrected it to
  Girons.

## Why nothing is in the 1567 transcription or the AD31 civil registers
The digitised 1B registers 1518-1587 are **civil** arrêts only; the 24 May 1567 sentence is criminal
(Tournelle). It is also not in BnF fr. 5455, which covers Nov–Dec 1567 Toulouse affairs only.
