# "Tilladet": the Cassagnet brothers, captains under Blaise de Monluc

Research notes compiled 7 October 2026. French quotations are verbatim from the OCR of the editions cited. Some OCR errors have been corrected silently, for example *ſ/f* and broken hyphenation. Every identification that is not explicit in a source is marked **[uncertain]**.

---

## 0. Key finding: "Tilladet" is two men

Sixteenth-century sources use "Tilladet" for **two brothers** of the Gascon house of **Cassagnet**:

| | **Antoine de Cassagnet**, elder brother | **François de Cassagnet**, younger brother |
|---|---|---|
| Usual name | "M. de Tilladet", "Tilladet l'aisné", "le capitaine Tilladet" | "M. de Saint-Orens / Sainct Orens / Sainct-Torains", "Tilladet le jeune", "le jeune Tilladet", "Tilladet de Saint-Orens" |
| Lordships | Tilladet, Cassagnet, Caussens (Gers) | Saint-Orens, La Roque (Larroque-Fimarcon, through his wife) |
| Main posts | Governor of Verrue (1555). Governor or commander of Bordeaux 1567/68 to Feb. 1569. Chevalier de Saint-Michel. Gentilhomme de la chambre | **Colonel (mestre de camp) of the légionnaires / légion de Guyenne**. Sénéchal of Bazadais. Capitaine of 50 hommes d'armes. Chevalier de l'Ordre |
| End | Shot in the belly at the escalade of **Mont-de-Marsan, 20 Sept. 1569**. Died two days later | Alive in 1577 (siege of Mirande). Made his will **13 April 1588**. Buried in Condom cathedral |

Monluc himself makes the distinction in one letter to Charles IX (Agen, 3 Feb. 1569). He sends 15 companies of foot under "le cappitaine Tilladet le jeune" and in the same letter asks for a company of gens d'armes for "le sieur de Tilladet l'aisné" (Ruble V, 142–148).

**Consequence for the 1567 Couserans episode.** The family-history note used so far says the Couserans commander was Antoine. That note copies La Chenaye-Desbois's article on Antoine (see §1). It merges the two brothers: Verrue, Saint-Michel, Bordeaux and Mont-de-Marsan belong to **Antoine**, while the colonelcy of the légion de Guyenne belongs to **François (Saint-Orens)**.

The only contemporary document that names the commander in Couserans is the 1567 petition of the Comminges towns. It names **"le seigneur de Sainct Orens, colonel de la légion de Guienne"** (Lestrade 1900, pp. 4–5). Monluc's ordinance on the same petition calls him "cappitaine Tilhadet". Lestrade, Bardies (1903) and the Revue de Comminges all call him "Tilladet de Saint-Orens".

So **the 1567 Couserans/Comminges pacifier was most probably François de Cassagnet, seigneur de Saint-Orens ("Tilladet le jeune"), not Antoine.** The Pamiers *Discours* and Crespin say only "capitaine Tilladet" / "sieur de Tilladet". Nothing positively places Antoine in Couserans, but the attribution to François rests on a single explicit document, so it is not absolutely certain.

## 1. Identity and family

### Name and arms
- Family: **Cassagnet**, from the "Sale noble de Cassagnet" in Armagnac, diocese of Auch, juridiction of Gondrin. Arms: *d'azur à la bande d'or* (Noulens, *Maisons historiques de Gascogne*, 1863, p. 24 n. 2).
- Spellings found: Tilladet, Tilhadet, Thilladet, Tiladet. Saint-Orens appears as Sainct Orens, Sainct Orenx, Sainctorens, Saint-Thorens, Sainct-Torains, Saint-Orans.

### Ancestry (La Chenaye-Desbois, *Dictionnaire de la noblesse*, 3rd ed., art. "Cassagnet", cols. 745–748; Gallica bpt6k54249441, views 336–337)
1. **Pons**, seigneur de Cassagnet. Did homage on 30 Nov. 1411 and again on 8 Jan. 1457.
2. **Sans**, seigneur de Cassagnet. Will dated 8 Feb. 1467. Wife: Bourguine de Verduzan.
3. **Manaud**, seigneur de Cassagnet. Married 10 June 1484 Agnès de Lasseran-de-Massencome.
4. **Bertrand**, seigneur de Cassagnet. Made purchases on 20 March 1512 and 29 Oct. 1518. He married **Marguerite du Bouzet** (OCR "Boulet"), *dame de la Sale de Tilladet* in the juridiction of Gondrin, of Roquas and of Pomasan. Her will is dated **2 Nov. 1523**. She was the daughter of "Antoine" du Bouzet and Catherine des Bordes.
   - Noulens corrects the father's name to **Jean II du Bouzet**, who married Catherine de Bordes in 1472. He adds that Marguerite first married a seigneur de Tilladet, held the *salle* of Tilladet at the end of the 15th century, and then remarried Bertrand de Cassagnet (Noulens 1863, pp. 24–25; Gallica bpt6k5406179j, views 33–34). That is how the Cassagnet came to hold Tilladet.
   - Children of Bertrand and Marguerite: **Antoine**, **François**, **Catherine**, and **Paule-Louise**, who married Bertrand de Baylens, baron de Poyanne.

### Antoine de Cassagnet, seigneur de Tilladet (the elder)
- **Titles:** seigneur de Tilladet, de Cassagnet and de Caussens (Ruble III, 93 n. 2; Bordenave ed. Raymond, p. 388 n. 2; Noulens p. 123).
- **Birth:** not recorded. His mother's will is dated 1523 and his brother is called "le jeune Tilladet" in the Piedmont wars of the 1540s, so Antoine was probably born in the first quarter of the 16th century, before 1523. This is an inference; no source gives a date. **[uncertain]**
- **Wife:** **Jeanne de Bezolles** (Noulens p. 25 n. 2), written "Jeanne de Bresolles" by La Chenaye. Bezolles is a Gers lordship, so Noulens's form is probably correct. **[uncertain]**
- **Son:** **Bernard de Cassagnet**, seigneur de Tilladet. La Chenaye says he was born in 1555.
  - On 5 Aug. 1589 Henri IV gave him a company of the Gardes. He became gentilhomme ordinaire de la chambre and governor of Bourg-sur-Mer. Brantôme V, 168–169 says Roquelaure placed "le seigneur de Thilladet" in Bourg.
  - He served in 1622 and died of plague at Béziers.
  - His second marriage, by contract of 19 Sept. 1588, was to Jeanne de Narbonne, daughter of Bernard, marquis de Fimarcon. Noulens, *Maison de Galard* IV/2, also gives 19 Sept. 1588.
  - Bernard is the great-grandfather's link to the 17th-century "marquis de Tilladet", cousins of Louvois. His son **Gabriel** (died 1660) married Madeleine Le Tellier, Louvois's sister. Gabriel's sons included **Jean-Baptiste, marquis de Tilladet** (wounded at Steenkerque, died 22 Aug. 1692), **Gabriel, chevalier de Tilladet** (died 1702) and **Michel**, bishop of Mâcon. Another son of Bernard, **Paul-Antoine** (died 1664), was marquis de Fimarcon through his wife.
  - Chronology check: Bernard born 1555 fits a father born before 1523 who married in the early 1550s. It also fits the 1588 and 1589 dates.
- **Religion:** Catholic, a royal commander. In 1562 at Condom he is credited with dividing the church between Catholics (the choir) and Protestants (the nave). Ruble III, 93 n. 2 cites Bèze; Bèze's index, however, gives this to "TILLADET, N. sieur de Saint-Orans, dit le capitaine". **[which brother is uncertain]**
- **Death:** wounded at Mont-de-Marsan on 20 Sept. 1569 and died "dans deux jours après", i.e. about 22 Sept. 1569 (see §4).

### François de Cassagnet, seigneur de Saint-Orens ("Tilladet le jeune", the younger)
- **Titles:** seigneur de Saint-Orens and de La Roque; chevalier de l'Ordre du Roi; capitaine of 50 hommes d'armes; sénéchal of Bazadais (La Chenaye; Ruble II, 348 n. 1; Antras ed. Carsalade/Tamizey, note 112).
- **Marriage:** **Jeanne de Monlezun, dame de Larroque-Fimarcon** (Antras, note 112). La Chenaye says he married twice.
  - Two daughters by the first marriage. The elder, **Françoise de Cassagnet-Saint-Orens**, dame de Larroque-Fimarcon, married **Jacques du Lau**, baron de Lau, a capitaine of 50 hommes d'armes. Larroque was then called Larroque-du-Lau (Antras, note 144).
  - One natural son. The will left him 300 écus and his upkeep until he had 400 écus of income from benefices (La Chenaye).
- **Will and burial:** will dated 13 April 1588; buried in the cathedral of Condom (La Chenaye; Antras note 112). Exact date of death unknown, on or after 13 April 1588. **[uncertain]**
- **Religion:** Catholic royal commander.

### Mistakes in the secondary literature
- **Ruble, Commentaires I, 181 n. 1, and the index entry "Tilladet (Bernard de), s. de Saint-Orens":** Ruble names the "jeune Tilladet qui à présent est seigneur de Sainct Orenx" of the Piedmont wars "Bernard de Tilladet". This is impossible, because Bernard was born in 1555. In II, 348 n. 1 Ruble correctly identifies the same man as François.
- **Ruble on Bordeaux:** Ruble is inconsistent about who governed Bordeaux. III, 93 n. 2 and V, 142 n. 1 name Antoine; III, 176 n. 1 and III, 234 n. 2 name "Tilladet de Saint-Orens". Two other sources support Antoine (see §2 under 1567–1569).
- **Gaullieur, *Histoire de la Réformation à Bordeaux* (1884), p. 434 n. 1:** calls Saint-Orens "Antoine de Tilladet, sieur de Saint-Orens", which merges the two brothers. His p. 341 n. 2 instead applies "Antoine" to "le jeune Tilladet" of 1562.
- **Lalanne's Brantôme:** the editor's note at t. IV, p. 86 identifies "le jeune Tilladet, maistre de camp des légionaires de Gascoigne" as Antoine, "tué devant Mont-de-Marsan". Brantôme himself (t. V, p. 184) says the colonel of the légionnaires was "M. de Thilladet le jeune, qu'on nommoit M. de Sainct-Torains". Lalanne's index also gives Antoine the governorship of Bourg-sur-Mer, which belonged to Bernard.
- **Bordenave, ed. P. Raymond, p. 309 n. 1:** says Saint-Orens's brother Tilladet was "tué en 1568". The year should be 1569.
- **Roquemaurel family-history site (degres_couserans, note 34):** says the Couserans "capitaine Tilladet" was Antoine and copies La Chenaye's career summary. See §0.
- **Bertrand de Cassagnet's wife:** La Chenaye has OCR "Marguerite de Boulet"; the correct name is du Bouzet.

## 2. Career chronology

Ruble = *Commentaires et lettres de Blaise de Monluc*, ed. A. de Ruble, 5 vols (1864–72). Volumes I–IV are the Commentaires plus letters up to 1564; volume V holds letters from 1565 and the index. "A" means Antoine, "F" means François (Saint-Orens). Where the source does not say which brother, the attribution follows Ruble's index and is marked **[uncertain]**.

### Italian wars
- **c. 1543–44, Piedmont:**
  - "le vieux Tilladet" commands 30 *salades* of M. de Termes's company in the action of the sieur d'Aussun (Ruble I, 187). Ruble's index gives this to A. **[uncertain]**
  - At Savigliano, "le cappitaine Tilladet ... n'estoict encores revenu à Savillan" (I, ~208).
  - "le june Tilhadet" leads a troop with Mons (I, 209), and "Tilhadet, Lavit, Idron, Monserié..." charge (I, ~214).
- **Same period:** "le jeune Tilladet qui à présent est seigneur de Sainct Orenx" (= F) and captain Ydron, with 25 *salades*, charge M. de la Trinitat's horse. The young Tilladet chases the fugitives to within two arquebus shots of Fossano and captures a flag (I, 181–183). Ruble II, 348 n. 1: F "avait fait ses premières armes en Italie, sous les ordres de Monluc".
- **1550:** Monluc and "Tilladet, qui avoict aussi eu conged" go to court. Henri II orders the two captains to join Brissac in Piedmont (I, 327–328; Courteault, *Blaise de Monluc historien*, 1908, p. 187). Ruble's index: A.
- **1551–52:** at the siege of Lanzo, the marshal sends "mandement au cappitaine Tilladet, à Savillan, de s'avancer nuict et jour" (I, 353). Index: A.
- **1555:** A is governor of Verrue in Piedmont (Ruble III, 93 n. 2; La Chenaye; Noulens).

### First civil war, 1561–1563
- **Late 1561:** Monluc raises 200 arquebusiers and 100 argoulets and gives "la charge au june Tilladet, qu'est aujourd'huy monsieur de Sainct Orens" (= F) (Ruble II, 348; Gaullieur p. 339). Ruble's index: "commande la première compagnie levée en Guyenne contre les huguenots". The Commentaires add:
  - Astaffort: F arrests the "séditieux", including Morelet-Lauzette (II, 362). They were executed at Villeneuve around 2 March 1562 (Courteault, *Un cadet de Gascogne*, 1909, p. 171; *Blaise de Monluc historien*, p. 416).
  - Layrac: F attacks the huguenots (II, 415).
  - Targon: F opens the attack (II, 432).
- **Jan. 1562:** Burie writes to Monluc "par le capitaine Tilladet". Gaullieur pp. 341–342 has "le jeune Tilladet" carrying Burie's letter, but his note says Antoine. **[uncertain]**
- **c. 1562, Condom:** "capitaine Tilladet" commands at Condom. He shares the church between the two religions, according to Bèze (Ruble III, 93 n. 2). Ruble gives this to A; Bèze's index gives it to "sieur de Saint-Orans". **[uncertain]**
- **6–11 March 1562, Fumel:** Burie and Monluc arrive at Fumel with "Tilladet" (II, 367 n. 1). Letter of Burie and Monluc, Fumel, 11 March 1561 o.s. (= 1562): "laissons en ceste ville six conseillers d'Agen avec le cappitaine Tilladet, pour parachever les procès des prisonniers" (IV, 124 and 127). Ruble: A ("pour parfaire les exécutions").
- **May 1562, Toulouse troubles:** Monluc enters the city with "cinquante arquebuziers à cheval du cappitaine Tilladet" (IV, 135–136). He must pay "celle du cappitaine Tilhadet" (IV, 140). The letters are dated Toulouse, 22 May 1562. **[brother uncertain]**
- **Summer 1562:**
  - Monluc watches Agen with "les arquebusiers de Tilladet" (Courteault 1908, pp. 434–435).
  - Tilladet is among the seven ensigns brought to Bordeaux in early July 1562 (Courteault 1908, pp. 436–437; Commentaires).
  - F is at the first siege of Montauban (II, 410).
- **Late summer to Oct. 1562:**
  - F is sent at full speed to secure Cahors, so that the huguenots find "les argoulets de M. de Saint-Orens" on the far bank of the Lot (III, 1–2; Gaullieur p. 434).
  - After Lectoure capitulates, F is sent back to Cahors (III, 25).
- **End of Oct. 1562:** "le capitaine Tilladet avec cent hommes" is sent to garrison Sainte-Foy and is later withdrawn (Courteault 1908, pp. 466–467 n. 5, from the jurades of Sainte-Foy). **[brother uncertain]**

### Between the wars, 1563–1567
- **Feb.–April 1563:** letters of Rascalon, valet de chambre du roi, "au capitaine Tilladet" about demolishing the fortifications of Millau (Doat 145, f. 237 ff., cited by Ruble V, 18 n. 1).
- **15 March 1565, Montauban:** Monluc to the queen: "Le capitaine Tilladet est icy, qui m'a asseuré de sa vie et de son honneur que à Saint Anthony ne y a rien à desmolir" (V, 18).
- **23 Jan. 1565, Toulouse:** Monluc to the king about "la charge que vous donnastes au capitaine Tilladet pour me dire" (V, 2). Ruble's index: A, "chargé d'écarter le trop grand nombre de gentilshommes assemblés à Toulouse".
- **12 July 1565, Estillac:** "présent le capitaine Tilladet", the Turkish ambassador speaks to Monluc about the siege of Malta. Monluc suggests "commander au dict capitaine Tilladet faire la messagerie" to court (V, 37–38).
- **Dec. 1565:** F negotiates Monluc's arrangement over the bishopric of Condom with Morvillier (V, 43 n. 2). Monluc asks the bishop of Condom to bring "Saint-Orens et de Pont" to the Estates of Agen (V, 43).
- **5 June 1566, Périgueux:** Monluc sends "le cappitaine Tilladet, présent porteur" to the queen with instructions. He asks that the captain be reimbursed 300 écus and that "aulx capitaines de sa légion" be assigned a year's pay (V, 57). Ruble's index gives this to A. The mention of "sa légion" might point to F, the legion's colonel. **[uncertain]**
- **8 July 1566, Agen:** "suivant ce qu'il vous a pleu me mander par le cappitaine Tilladet" (V, 60–61, two documents).
- **14 Feb. 1567 – Dec. 1567: Couserans/Comminges pacification and the Cabannes raid.** See §3. Most probably F.
- **8 April 1567:** at the marriage contract of Brandelise du Bouzet and Jean II de Faudoas, the bride's relatives present include "Antoine de Cassagnet, seigneur de Tilladet et de Caussens" and "François de Cassagnet, seigneur de Saint-Orens" (Noulens 1863, pp. 122–123; Gallica bpt6k5406179j, views 131–132). The brothers were related to the du Bouzet through their mother.

### Second and third civil wars, 1567–1570
- **20–29 Sept. 1567:**
  - On Saturday 20 Sept., Monluc leaves for the baths of Barbotan with Robert de Gontaut, bishop of Condom, and "les seigneurs de Saint Orens et de Tilladet, frères" (Ruble III, 93; Courteault 1909, p. 231: "les deux Tilladet").
  - On Sunday 28 Sept., Saint-Orens visits Monluc at Cassaigne to go hawking. The news of the huguenot rising (the *Michelade*) arrives that night (III, 97 and 101; Courteault 1908, pp. 498–499).
  - On 29 Sept. Monluc sends captains "à la maison de monsieur de Sainct Orens ... qu'il ... advertist monsieur de Tilladet, son frère" (III, 101–102; OCR running heads read "401/402", but these are pp. 101–102).
- **4 Oct. 1567:** a letter of "Tilladet de Saint-Orens" to the consuls of Condom announces the arrival of their new governor (Ruble III, 297 n., from the reg. cons. de Condom).
- **9 Oct. 1567, Agen:** Monluc's commission to "Tilladet de Saint-Orens, colonel des légions de Guyenne, pour lever deux compagnies de gens de pied, chacune de 300 hommes". Original in Arch. nat., K 94 (Ruble V, 336). In the Commentaires, Monluc "baillay les gens de pied à monsieur de Saint Orens, que estoit collonnel des légionnaires" (III, 118, n. 2 dates Saint-Orens's commissions to 9 Oct.). François d'Esparbès de Lussan had a commission "de trois cents hommes du régiment de Tilladet le 13 décembre 1567" (Ruble III, c. 103–105 n. 2).
- **Nov. 1567, the relief army sent north to the king:**
  - Monluc gives Saint-Orens "les gens de pied" and sees them leave Limoges (III, 121–124). Monsalès leads the vanguard and Terride commands.
  - *Revue de Gascogne* 39 (1898), p. 396 n. 2 (Gallica bpt6k5726285w, view 406): Monluc, at Limoges 9–12 Nov. 1567, "assista au départ des troupes qu'il envoyait au secours du roi sous les ordres de Terride, Monsalès et Tilladet de Saint-Orens ... Ce départ eut lieu le 12". On 13 Nov. Monluc was at Saint-Junien.
- **Late 1567, combat of Champoly/Champouilly (Auvergne, near Feurs):** Montsalès defeats Poncenat. Brantôme: "M. de Thilladet le jeune, qu'on nommoit M. de Sainct-Torains, qui estoit couronnel des légionnaires de Gascoigne, un très-brave et vaillant capitaine, acquist là beaucoup d'honneur" (Brantôme, *Œuvres*, ed. Lalanne, V, 184). See also IV, 86: "le jeune Tilladet, maistre de camp des légionaires de Gascoigne, fort brave et vaillant capitaine, qui nous menoit". Brantôme was in this force.
- **1567–1569, Bordeaux:** the brother concerned is most probably A.
  - Ruble III, 93 n. 2: "En 1568, par ordre du roi (pièce datée du 8 février 1568; coll. Harlay St-G., vol. 320, 5, f° 191), Tilladet devint gouverneur de Bordeaux."
  - *Archives historiques de la Gironde* (Bordeaux chronicle, document no. CIII, p. 255; Gallica bpt6k34112t, view 297): "Le lundy VIIe febvrier 1569, monsieur de Montferrant fust reçeu par la court comme gouverneur de ceste ville ... et, le jour auparavant, le sieur de Tilhiadet qui auparavant estoit gouverneur s'en alla." The editor's note: "Antoine de Cassagnet, sieur dudit lieu et de Tilladet, nommé gouverneur de la ville et pays de Bordeaux par Monluc, en 1567, mort en 1569 au siége de Mont-de-Marsan." That volume's chronological table reads "Charles de Montferrand est reçu comme gouverneur de Bordeaux en remplacement d'Antoine de Tilladet".
  - Conflicts with the jurats, the mayor and the parlement led to his replacement (Ruble V, 131 n. 1 and V, 141 n. 1, citing Devienne, *Hist. de Bordeaux*, pp. 152 ff.).
  - Monluc defends him: "Et croys que le faict de monsieur de Tilladet ne porte autre chose que cela, car je l'excuseray tousjours que faulte de bonne volunté ne l'a pas gardé qu'il n'aye contenté tout le monde ... car il est homme de service" (letter to the king, Agen, 3 Feb. 1569; V, 142).
  - In the same letter Monluc asks that the late Francisco d'Este's company of gens d'armes be given "au sieur de Tilladet l'aisné" (V, 147–148).
  - Ruble III, 234 n. 2 and III, 176 n. 1 instead name "Tilladet de Saint-Orens", saying the chevalier de Monluc succeeded him as colonel from Feb. 1568 because he had become governor of Bordeaux. The Bordeaux chronicle and Monluc's "l'aisné" favour Antoine. **[conflict in Ruble's notes]**
- **Feb.–April 1568:**
  - Feb. 1568: the chevalier de Monluc becomes colonel of the légionnaires de Guyenne. Some of the captains "amenés par Tilladet" desert the royal camp with their soldiers' pay (Ruble III, 176 n. 1 and III, 177 n. 1).
  - 2 April 1568, Saint-Jean-d'Angély: Monluc asks the king for the collar of the Order for, among others, "de Tilladet le jeune" (V, 116).
- **31 Oct. 1568, Villeneuve-d'Agen:** "J'ay layssé le cappitaine Tilladet à Agen et en ces cartiers de delà, parce que les viscontes ne sont point partys du pays" (V, 135). Ruble's index: F.
- **Feb. 1569:** "J'ay envoyé quinze enseignes de gens de pied, le cappitaine Tilladet le jeune, avec sa compaignie et la mienne, aussi entre les dictes deux rivières" (Lot and Dordogne) (V, 142–143).
- **Spring 1569:**
  - Monluc recalls "Saint-Orens" with his company against Piles (III, 219).
  - After Terride leaves for Béarn, "demeurasmes monsieur de Saint-Orens et moy" at Sainte-Foy (III, 230).
  - F is placed in Libourne "avec sa companye de gens d'armes" (III, 208).
  - His house is pillaged by the huguenots (III, 215).
  - He is recalled by the duc d'Anjou (III, 254).
- **20 Sept. 1569, Mont-de-Marsan:** A is mortally wounded (see §4).
- **1570:**
  - F is maréchal de camp of Monluc's army for the invasion of Béarn (III, 400) and joins Monluc at Nogaro (III, 404).
  - After Monluc is wounded at Rabastens, a commission dated Rabastens, 25 July 1570, delegates command "aux sieurs de Gondrin, Tilladet de Saint-Orens, de Gohas et de Madaillan" (V, 341; *Revue de Comminges* t. XI). Bordenave says Monluc left the army to "Montespan et Saint-Thorens", who summoned the château of Montaner (Bordenave, *Histoire de Béarn et Navarre*, ed. P. Raymond, 1873, p. 309).
  - F is charged with blocking Montamat's passage (V, 280). In 1570 he was sent to ask the governor of Fuenterrabía for Spanish help; the mission failed (Ruble II, 348 n. 1, citing coll. Harlay St-Germ. 323).
  - F is proposed by Monluc for the Order of Saint-Michel (V, 116) and receives the chancellor at Toulouse (IV, 372).

### Later career of François
- **1575:** "mestre de camp dans l'armée de Monluc, alors maréchal de France" (Ruble II, 348 n. 1, citing the reg. cons. d'Agen).
- **1577:** among the Catholic lords called to retake **Mirande**: "de Sainctorens (112)" (Antras, *Mémoires*, p. 60, with note 112 on p. 153).
- **13 April 1588:** will; burial in Condom cathedral.

## 3. The 1567 Couserans/Comminges pacification and the Cabannes/Tachard episode

### 3.1 The quarrel
Monluc's letter to Charles IX, Agen, 14 Feb. 1567 (Ruble V, no. 160, pp. 77–83) describes the following:
- The "jeune Fontraille", brother of the sénéchal d'Armagnac, pillages the Haut-Comminges ("Il n'a aucune religion, car autant de pilleries fait-il sur les ungs que sur les autres").
- For six months he has been allied with his brother-in-law **the sieur de Solan** (Jean-François de Mauléon, vicomte de Soulan), the Protestant leader at Pamiers after the 1566 massacre.
- They quarrel with the **Roquemaurel**, whom Monluc calls Protestants, over a bird of prey ("pour ung oyseau").
- "depuis douze jours en çà les dicts Fontraille et Solan ont surprins de nuict le jeune Rocquemaurel en sa maison ... bruslé la maison, le dict Rocquemaurel et six autres personnes". The house is the château de Soueix and the victim Arnaud de Roquemaurel, which dates the attack to about 2 Feb. 1567.
- Fourteen or fifteen hundred men are under arms. Solan's side holds **Saint-Girons**, half of which belongs to the vicomte de Fimarcon. The Roquemaurel are in a royal town (Saint-Lizier).

Monluc's request (pp. 81–82) is to go with horse, foot and artillery, and:
> "commander au cappittaine Tilladet de lever troys ou quatre cens hommes, car ne fault faire levées de gens de ce pays de delà, parce qu'ilz sont presque tous parens d'une part ou d'autre"

Ruble's note at this point refers to "le récit de l'expédition de Tilladet" in the *Archives curieuses*, t. VI.

On 31 March 1567 (V, 83–86) Monluc asks the capitouls of Toulouse for two cannon to carry out the king's commission. In the same letter: "Nous vivons en paix par toute ceste Guyenne, sans ceste querelle particulière, qui est au hault Commenge, où le Roy m'envoya."

Courteault (*Un cadet de Gascogne*, 1909, pp. 230–231; the OCR running head reads "331"): "en mai, il fut obligé d'aller lui-même, avec trois compagnies de gens d'armes et quatre cents arquebusiers, réprimer de ce côté les exactions de deux audacieux partisans, Fontrailles et Solan."

### 3.2 The troops and the commander
- The *Discours des troubles de Pamiers* (*Archives curieuses*, 1st series, t. VI, pp. 341–342): Monluc "leva trois cents harquebusiers sous la charge du capitaine Tilladet".
- The petition of the Comminges towns (see 3.3) names "le seigneur de Sainct Orens, colonel de la légion de Guienne".
- The roll of 4 April 1568 names two other captains, **Alexandre** and **Ampela** (Lestrade 1900, p. 43). Captain Alexandre's company was fed at Le Plan and Samatan; Ampela passed through Salies.
- Monluc himself came: the roll records expenses "aud. s[ieu]r de Monluc avec son train" at Aurignac, Saint-Lizier and Muret, where he held the *montre*.
- Lestrade (p. 46) lists other captains crossing the Comminges in 1567: "Tilladet de Saint-Orens, Parron, Malvoisin, Alexandre". This may cover the autumn levies as well.
- Garrisons were afterwards left in Saint-Girons and Saint-Lizier (Lestrade p. 10).

### 3.3 The levies on the Comminges châtellenies
**Petition** of the consuls of Muret, Samatan, Aurignac, L'Isle-en-Dodon "et aultres du pays de Comenge", signed "Pontic, scindic". The original is in the Archives de Muret, liasse 40 / "I. Registre des États"; it is printed in Lestrade, *Les Huguenots en Comminges* (1900), pp. 3–6, and in the *Revue de Comminges* t. X.
- It says that, on the king's order, Monluc had companies led to Saint-Girons and Saint-Lizier "par le seigneur de Sainct Orens, colonel de la légion de Guienne, lequel soy-disant commissaire par vous depputé auroict procédé à la cottisation de certains vivres et munitions".
- The assessment was "la chastelenie d'Aurignac cent cestiers de bled, cinquante motons, quinze pipes de vin, et le semblable sur les chastelenies de Muret, Samathan, l'Isle en Dodon et aultres". It was enforced "à la poyne de cinq cens livres, et de prison".
- Many people of the Comminges were imprisoned and goods seized at the suit of the syndics of Saint-Girons and Saint-Lizier.
- The petitioners asked to be discharged, or that any reimbursement fall "sur les biens desd. de Solan et Roquemaurel".

**Monluc's ordinance**, Cassaigne, **11 July 1567**, signed "De Monluc" and countersigned "Lauzit":
- The heavy expense "n'a esté faicte et moins cottizée par nostre consentement".
- Both sides are to appear with "tous les estatz et comissions par eulz obtenus de Nous et du cappitaine Tilhadet, fraiz et despence par eulz faictz, depuis nostre venue ausd. lieuz de Sainct Girons et Sainct Lizier, jusques au partement dud. cappitaine Tilhadet".
- The prisoners are to be released and the seized goods returned at once, on pain of 10,000 livres.

**Aurignac, 7 July 1567:** the consuls write to Pontic, syndic of the Tiers État, that they dare not go to fairs and markets "pour craintre d'estre prins et arrestés ... ainsin que journellement les de Sainct Gyrons en preignent" (Lestrade pp. 6–7).

**Agen, 7 Sept. 1567:** arbitration before Monluc between Dominique Pontic and the syndics of Saint-Girons, Couserans, Lescure and Montbrun. Saint-Girons argued from letters of Henri II given at Blois in 1555 that it had no tie to the Comminges. Monluc referred the matter to the next Estates. Source: the Roquemaurel family-history site (degres_couserans), citing "Lestrade, op. cité". **I could not find this text in the 1900 Lestrade volume I searched; it may be in another Lestrade series or in the Muret registers. [unverified]**

**Settlement:**
- **December 1567:** by an accord drawn up by Gailliard Verniolle, notary at Muret, the Estates of Comminges granted **600 livres tournois** to Saint-Girons, the vicomté of Couserans and Lescure. The Estates sat at Muret from 16 to 22 Dec. 1567.
- **4 April 1568:** Monluc commissioned D. de Borderia, juge de Comminges. With the commissioners A. Cambornac, B. Gabalby and D. Saint-Pierre he drew up the "Rolle de la Taxe, vérification et modération" of supplies furnished. Examples: Saint-Lizier 955 l. for Monluc's table; Fronsac 452 l. 12 s.; Aspet 442 l.; Castillon 405 l. 4 s.; Salies 223 l. 1 s. 6 d.; Muret 200 l. for Monluc's *montre*. The total was **4,017 l. 12 s. 6 d.** (Lestrade pp. 42–45, from the Archives de Muret, Estates at L'Isle-en-Dodon, July 1568).
- Bardies (1903) and the family-history site give 3,964 livres. That may be the total before the 600 l. item or a different computation. **[uncertain]**

**Judgement of the parlement of Toulouse, 24 May 1567:**
- Soulan, Marestaing, Costans *dit* capitaine Villeneuve and Jean-Jacques were sentenced to the wheel; others to be hanged.
- The château of Lescure was to be razed.
- Gabrielle d'Espinas (Roquemaurel's widow) was to receive damages.

### 3.4 Les Cabannes and the minister Tachard

**Discours des troubles advenus en la ville de Pamiers** (*Archives curieuses de l'histoire de France*, 1re série, t. VI, pp. 341–342):
> "il advint que le sieur de Monluc fut commandé pour faire poser les armes à deux gentilshommes du conté de Foix, asçavoir du Solan et Roquemaurel, qui avoyent querelle ensemble, et, pour ce faire, il leva trois cents harquebusiers sous la charge du capitaine Tilladet. Quand ils eurent exécuté leur commission, ce Roquemaurel dressa une entreprise avec eux contre ceux de Pamiers qui s'estoyent retirez aux Cabanes, donnant à entendre que les bandoliers qui avoyent suivi le parti de son ennemy estoyent avec eux."

The rest of the passage says:
- The Pamiers refugees had been ordered by Jeanne d'Albret to leave the county of Foix for Béarn. About thirty of them stayed at Les Cabannes with "M. Martin Tachart, jadis ministre au Val d'Angrogne et depuis à Montauban".
- They were surprised at dawn on **26 May 1567** by "quatre ou cinq cents arquebuziers et quarante chevaux". Two soldiers and one of the refugees were killed, three were taken prisoner, and the houses were pillaged.
- Tachard fled but was taken by two peasants: "Tilladet le mena en triomphe par Foix, avec un bonnet blanc et des patenostres au col, et se retirèrent chez l'évesque de Couzerans".
- A rumour of ransom was spread to prevent a rescue, "car ils le menèrent par eau à Tholose, où, après longues procédures, le parlement l'a fait exécuter".

**Crespin, *Histoire des martyrs*** (ed. Toulouse 1885–89, t. III, p. 648; Gallica bpt6k11969085, views 666–667), with more detail and different dates:
- The commissioners gathered "iusques à 500 hommes ... y estans pour chefs & conducteurs le sieur de Tilladet & autres Capitaines".
- They reached Les Cabannes on the morning of **25 May 1567**.
- Tachard was brought "audit Tilladet, lequel s'en estant saisi le traicta en toute cruauté & derision ... le fit marcher par mocquerie ayant un chapeau blanc en teste, portant de grosses patenostres à son col".
- Other prisoners were ransomed, but Tachard was taken "par le commandement dudit Tilladet à Thoulouse avec quatre autres prisonniers, un vendredi 6 de juin".
- He was interrogated on 8 June. His recusations were rejected on 28 June and 4 July. He appeared before the two chambers on 5 July.
- Sentence: "Prononcé à Thoulouse en Parlement le sixiesme iour de Iuillet M.D.LXVII". He was hanged in the place Saint-Georges and left on the gibbet until noon the next day.

**Olhagaray**, *Histoire des comtes de Foix, Béarn et Navarre* (1629), p. ~561: "Les ministres Tachard et d'Engays, Ramond la Parre furent faits prisonniers, et menez au capitaine Tilladet". Quoted in Castillon d'Aspet, *Histoire du comté de Foix* II, pp. 222–225, which gives **25 May 1567** and the hearing of 5 July. The same quotation is in D. Benoit, *Les origines de la Réforme à Montauban* (1910), pp. 83–84.

The Roquemaurel site (ustou.txt) says **Girons de Roquemaurel d'Ustou**, the murdered man's brother, went with Tilladet to Les Cabannes.

**Which Tilladet?** None of the Pamiers or Foix narratives adds a forename. The commander of the Couserans companies was the colonel Saint-Orens (§3.2), and the Cabannes raid was carried out by the same troops "quand ils eurent exécuté leur commission". So the "Tilladet" of Les Cabannes was most probably **François, seigneur de Saint-Orens**. **[probable, not explicit]**

## 4. Other notable episodes

- **Monluc's prophetic dream, Sept. 1567.** On the trip to Barbotan with "les seigneurs de Saint Orens et de Tilladet, frères", Monluc dreams at Panjas's house of the coming rising. He gives up the baths, and a week later the *Michelade* breaks out (Ruble III, 93–97).
- **Mont-de-Marsan, 20 Sept. 1569. Antoine's death.**
  - Monluc had sent "monsieur de Tilladet" back to the maréchal de Damville to argue for crossing the river. On his return Tilladet found the argoulets hanging back behind the houses. He "vint bas à course de cheval, et les fist ouster de dernier des maisons ... se mettant à gallopper au long du fossé pour donner couraige aux argolletz". Then "une harquebousade luy donna dans le ventre; son cheval tumba, et luy se sauva tout blessé plus de cent pas ... et dans deux jours après il mourut de ce coup" (Ruble III, 318–320).
  - Bordenave, *Histoire de Béarn et Navarre*, p. 388, says the garrison was massacred "en despit de ce que Tilladet, maistre de camp de l'infanterie de Monluc, avoit esté tué d'une harquebusade à l'entrée". The editor's note identifies him as Antoine, "frère aîné de Saint-Orens".
  - Courteault (1908, pp. 556–557) dates the escalade **20 Sept. 1569**, so Antoine died about **22 Sept. 1569**. Ruble's note gives the reconnaissance of the place as "1[8?] septembre 1569" from Aubais. La Chenaye says only "blessé devant Mont-de-Marsan".
  - Bordenave's title "maistre de camp de l'infanterie" for Antoine sits awkwardly with Saint-Orens as colonel of the légionnaires. This may reflect the 1569 arrangements. **[uncertain]**
- **Saint-Orens and Jeanne d'Albret, 1568.** Monluc's wife, Saint-Orens and Monluc's sons went to Nérac to run at the ring with the young prince of Navarre. The queen left Nérac the same Sunday (Ruble III, c. 176).

## 5. Later life and death

- **Antoine:** governor or commander of Bordeaux from 1567/68 until 6 Feb. 1569, then replaced by Charles de Montferrand. Wounded on 20 Sept. 1569 and died two days later at Mont-de-Marsan. Survived by his son Bernard (born 1555).
- **François (Saint-Orens):**
  - Colonel of the légionnaires until Feb. 1568 (Ruble III, 176 n. 1).
  - Maréchal de camp in the 1570 Béarn campaign.
  - Mestre de camp in 1575.
  - Present at the recapture of Mirande in 1577.
  - Sénéchal of Bazadais and chevalier de l'Ordre; the dates of these posts were not found.
  - Will 13 April 1588; buried in Condom cathedral.
  - His heir through his elder daughter was the house of du Lau (Larroque-du-Lau).

## 6. Open questions and leads

1. **Commander in Couserans, 1567.** The petition names Saint-Orens. Check the Muret originals (Arch. mun. Muret, liasse 40, registres des États) for any further naming. Look for Monluc's commission for the expedition, probably March–April 1567, which is not in Ruble.
2. **Agen arbitration of 7 Sept. 1567.** Locate the primary text, probably in the Archives de Muret, Lestrade's later "nouvelle série" fascicules (Gallica bpt6k96047673 and others), or the *Revue de Comminges*.
3. **Bordeaux governorship.** Settle Antoine against François using the royal order of 7/8 Feb. 1568 (BnF, coll. Harlay Saint-Germain fr. 320, 1 f. 244 and 320, 5 f. 191), Devienne's *Histoire de Bordeaux* pp. 152 ff., and the Bordeaux *registres secrets*. Most evidence points to Antoine.
4. **Order of Saint-Michel.** The *Recueil historique des chevaliers de l'Ordre de Saint-Michel* vol. III (1568–1574), (2003; the compiler's name is truncated in the Gallica record as "Jean Fr…"), should give exact dates for both brothers. It is not online.
5. **Exact dates for François** as sénéchal of Bazadais and capitaine of 50 lances, his first wife's name (La Chenaye says he married twice), and his date of death after April 1588. Lead: the notarial will of 13 April 1588 at Condom; Philippe Lauzun's study of the lords of Fimarcon in the *Bulletin de la Société archéologique du Gers*.

7. **"Vieux Tilladet" in Piedmont, 1540s.** Is he Antoine (the elder brother), or a father or uncle from Marguerite du Bouzet's first marriage to the seigneur de Tilladet? Unresolved.
